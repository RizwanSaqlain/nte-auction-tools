NTE Bidding Strategy for Going, Going, Gone

Start with the device you used in the game. The Nine-slot Average Valuation Device reports an average; the Gold-rarity Valuation Device reports a total. Select the corresponding tool in the workspace before entering the reading.

Nine-slot Device: Establish a Subtotal Baseline

Enter the average and use the item-count filter only when you know how many nine-slot collectibles are present. Several bundles can share the same average while having different total values. Use the lowest matching nine-slot subtotal as a conservative starting point under the selected catalog and tolerance.

The bid panel calculates buffered limit = lowest matching subtotal × (1 − buffer). Adjust the buffer and compare your planned bid. This is a planning aid for the nine-slot portion of the box, not a guarantee of profit or a prediction of other players’ bids.

Gold Device: Narrow the Possible Contents

Enter the total gold value exactly. Every returned bundle has that same total, so the search helps identify possible contents rather than a higher or lower gold valuation. Set copy, item, and slot limits, then add minimum quantities for any known gold collectibles.

The default limits are 5 copies per item, 25 items, and 140 slots. Increase them if your clues justify it, up to 250 total slots. Restrictive assumptions can exclude the actual contents. A no-match result means nothing matched within your chosen search limits and the supplied catalog; it does not prove the gadget reading is wrong.

Read Search Limits and Averages Carefully

Gold searches can stop at the result cap or time limit. Check the status before treating the list as complete. Average items and average slots summarize all returned bundles across every result page; when results are partial, the averages describe only that returned subset. They are not likelihood estimates.

Avoid Counting the Same Collectible Twice

Gold-rarity and nine-slot categories overlap: Asahi Inori Figurine and Wheelchair Model appear in both catalogs. Do not simply add both device values when deciding a bid. Other collectibles may add value, but these tools do not calculate the entire box, fees, modifiers, or the physical arrangement of item shapes.

Check the catalogs against your game version and report mismatched values. Keep your final bid within a limit you are comfortable with.

NTE Auction Tools FAQ

Which devices does the site support?

The Nine-slot Average Valuation Device and the Gold-rarity Valuation Device. Use the Device selector to switch their content inside the same homepage workspace. Tool and Catalog switch between the selected calculator and its items.

What is the difference between the two calculations?

The nine-slot tool matches an average against combinations of 8 collectibles in groups of 1–7. The gold tool matches an exact total against combinations from 50 gold collectibles with configurable search limits. Repeated items are allowed in both.

What does the nine-slot ±2 tolerance mean?

The calculator rounds your reading and each bundle’s average to whole numbers, then accepts a difference of up to 2 by default. You can change this tolerance. The gold tool uses exact sums and does not apply ±2.

Can a box contain only one nine-slot collectible?

Yes. The nine-slot tool includes single-item matches. If there is one nine-slot collectible, its average equals its item value.

Which constraints can I change for gold searches?

Maximum copies per item (1–25), total items (1–50), total slots (1–250), and returned results (1–2,000). Defaults are 5, 25, 140, and 300 respectively. The 250-slot ceiling is the box capacity; the other limits bound the search.

How do known gold items work?

Use +/− or enter a minimum quantity in the gold catalog. Increments are limited by the selected copy, item, slot, and total-value constraints. Run a new search after changing clues. Each returned bundle must include at least those quantities, and the required items are highlighted. If you lower a limit below your known quantities, correct the quantities or raise the limit before searching.

What do average items and average slots describe?

They are arithmetic averages across all bundles returned by the gold search, including other result pages. Each item’s quantity counts toward the averages. They do not predict the actual item count or occupied slots, especially when the search returned only partial results.

How does the gold search find combinations?

It uses depth-first backtracking with branch-and-bound pruning: try an item quantity, explore the remaining items, then backtrack to another choice. A precomputed maximum-value table rules out branches that cannot reach the target within the remaining count and slots. Known minimum quantities must also be satisfied. Read the algorithm explanation in About Us for more detail.

Why did the gold search stop early?

It reached your maximum result count or its time limit. The status marks partial results, and more combinations may exist. Narrow the constraints or add known items to reduce the search space. You can also cancel a running search.

Do slot limits check whether the items physically fit?

No. The gold tool sums occupied slots; it does not arrange item shapes or test whether they fit the box layout.

Can I add the values from both devices?

Not directly. A gold collectible can also occupy nine slots, so adding the values may double-count it. Each tool covers only its device’s category, not the full box.

Is this an official game service?

No. NTE Auction Tools is an independent fan project with no game-account connection or automated bidding. See About Us for its scope and Terms & Conditions for usage details.
